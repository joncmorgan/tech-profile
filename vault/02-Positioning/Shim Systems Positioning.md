# Shim Systems — Positioning

Shim Systems is a dormant company (sole director: Jon), reactivated as the
vehicle for boutique B2B work rather than left as an unused placeholder. This
doc captures where its positioning landed after working through the options,
so it doesn't have to be re-derived from scratch each time it comes up.

## The one-liner

> Decision, data and workflow systems for technical advisory teams working
> on complex building and climate-risk problems.

17 words, vertical-first. Narrow enough to be credible and referable off the
back of one real engagement; broad enough to expand later without a full
repositioning.

## Why this framing, and not broader or narrower

Perplexity research into brand architecture for boutique technical
consultancies framed the choice as a spectrum:

- **Too narrow**: "workflow automation for façade consultancies" — hard to
  say out loud, no room to grow into adjacent work.
- **Too broad**: "custom systems architecture for any growing organisation"
  — no proof point, sounds like every other systems consultancy, doesn't
  connect to 25+ years of actual domain depth.
- **Mid-band (chosen)**: named a real class of problem (climate/building
  risk) and a real class of buyer (technical advisory teams) without naming
  a single narrow niche or a single client type.

## Why vertical-first, not broad-first

Decided to lead narrow (Option A) rather than broad, on the logic that a
flagship engagement — JFS Engineers, or whichever one materialises first —
becomes the proof point the narrow positioning can point to, rather than
Shim Systems trying to sound credible with no case study behind it yet.

**Conditions for broadening beyond the vertical**, once met:
- Two substantial engagements, in genuinely different contexts (not two
  façade-engineering firms).
- A publishable case study from at least one of them.
- 20–30% of inbound enquiry already coming from outside the initial
  vertical, unprompted — i.e. broadening because the market is already
  pulling that way, not broadening speculatively ahead of evidence.

Until then, the vertical framing stays as-is rather than hedging it broader
"just in case."

## What Shim Systems is actually for, right now

Two concrete jobs, not a general-purpose consultancy shopfront:

1. **IP holding vehicle for BER-Engine.** The engine is architected as three
   pieces — an upstream ingestion tool (scans folders, splits PDFs, finds
   floor plans), Studio (interactive footprint authoring), and Engine
   (downstream report generation to the host firm's templates) — all held
   as Shim Systems IP, licensed (not sold) to whichever firm hosts the work
   (e.g. JFS Engineers). The host gets a non-exclusive licence to use it;
   Shim retains the underlying IP and can license it elsewhere.
2. **Commercial vehicle for a paid pilot engagement**, not an employment
   contract. This solves a specific problem in the JFS pitch: Pete may not
   be the sole decision-maker internally, so a bounded, priced commercial
   pilot (staged diagnostic → build-and-launch → paid market pilot, each
   stage ending in a go/no-go) is a lower-risk ask than "create a Director
   role for me" — something Pete can propose upward without having to win
   a headcount argument first.

## Where this sits relative to the CV/LinkedIn positioning

The CV/LinkedIn positioning (`CV-Positioning.md`) is Jon Morgan the
individual, pitched at Director/Principal-level employment. Shim Systems
positioning is the company vehicle, pitched at commercial engagements and
IP licensing. They're related — the same underlying expertise — but they're
not the same document and don't need to read identically: one is a resume
substitute, the other is closer to a capability statement for a business
that doesn't have a market track record yet.
