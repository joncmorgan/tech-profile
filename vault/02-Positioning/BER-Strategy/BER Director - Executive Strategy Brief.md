## Executive Summary & Core Mechanics

This is a pitch, not a strategy already in motion — a hypothesis for a new Technical Director role, built to take to a target firm and see what lands. Some of them may already sense the gap (JFS's Victorian Director, unprompted, said they'd been thinking about bringing ESD in-house), but almost certainly none have articulated it in this specific form, and none currently have anyone positioned to build it. The approach is to walk in with the thesis largely formed, not to wait for a firm to define the problem first.

The thesis has two standalone offerings — one for new-build work, one for existing buildings. Neither depends on the other; either could be pitched alone, and a target firm could adopt one without the other.

**Both share one production model, treated here as solved rather than open:** rapid geometry reconstruction and analysis, including AI support, brings the marginal cost of producing either offering's output close to zero. That doesn't mean close to zero _price_ to the client — pricing is a separate decision, made later — it means close to zero _Director time_ per output, at high quality. That's the actual cornerstone: a loss-leader mechanism only works at real volume if it doesn't consume the Director's own calendar, and both offerings below are built specifically around avoiding that trap.

**Why this gap exists, not just that it does:** none of the facade firms with adjacent ESD capability actually occupy this specific positioning. Three of them (see Target Companies below) built certification-administration practices — Green Star/NABERS/WELL, a different buyer answering a different question (voluntary market differentiation) than mandatory climate-risk disclosure. The one exception got there through structural engineering's own natural adjacency to embodied carbon — material tonnage recast through a carbon lens — not through facade-risk physics, and needed real headcount to assemble that synthesis from several overlapping specialists. Facade physical-risk positioning specifically needs one person who already holds both halves personally — building-physics/thermal expertise and climate-disclosure literacy together. A smaller firm doesn't need that firm's scale to get there; it needs to hire the one person who already is that combination, rather than trying to build it from parts.

## The Pre-Planning Advisory Brief (New-Build)

Position the firm as an upstream technical authority _before_ an ESD consultant is appointed. Rather than billable pre-DA consulting or low-ROI compliance simulations, the door-opener is a free or low-cost Brief, delivered to architects at concept stage. It establishes governing design boundaries without dictating architectural massing, and is never sold or labelled as ESD compliance work — even though the engine underneath runs the same physics.

```
[ Pre-Planning Advisory Brief ] (Engine-driven, near-zero Director time)
            │
            ├──► Part 1: Governing Parameters & System Rules
            │         └─► Establishes Technical Authority at Concept Stage
            │
            └──► Part 2: Forward Resilience Radar (Flagged Risks)
                      └─► Creates a Warm, Pre-Briefed Path to Design Scope:
                           • 42°C Overheating & Thermal Stress Analysis
                           • Extreme Rain & Box-Gutter Capacity
                           • Hail & High-Suction Structural Wind Loading
                           • Embodied Carbon in Extrusions
```

1. **Part 1: Practical Directional Guidance** What architects need before pencil hits paper: NCC Section J/JV3 compliance-grade thermal bounds — the only "governing standard" the firm actually speaks to directly, since it's mandatory and physics-based, not a voluntary scheme requiring separate accreditation — plus glazing spec bounds (VLT, tint), system-suite guidance (thermally broken dies vs. bespoke extrusions with lead-time warnings), budget-uplift indices, and massing-bounds tables by elevation (WWR%, feasible U-value/SHGC, shading/fin rules). Where a project happens to be targeting Green Star, NABERS, or WELL, the same physics numbers are usually what those schemes need satisfied anyway — worth a one-line note in the Brief that the bounds given will support that target, without the firm ever speaking to the certification pathway, the accreditation, or the submission itself.
2. **Part 2: The Forward Resilience Radar** Flags four physical risks that annual-compliance views ignore: peak 42°C heatwave overheating (2.1 design-day sizing), extreme rain/box-gutter overflow capacity, hail and wind-suction loading, and embodied carbon in aluminium extrusions — chosen because they sit outside standard JV3/NABERS scope, so no other early-stage consultant has likely raised them yet.
3. **Commercial Conversion** Defining technical parameters early earns trust and a seat at the table before scope is set. Unresolved risks in the Radar give the client a reason to retain the firm for detailed design, structural engineering, and remediation — directly on private jobs, and as a pre-briefed advantage into the tender on public ones.

**Two assumptions this offering depends on:** (1) that architects act on flagged risks by retaining the firm rather than taking the free brief to a cheaper compliance shop once it exists — unproven, and exactly what the first Brief should test; (2) "un-tendered" scope holds for privately procured work, but not for the public-sector share of the pipeline, which still runs through EOI/tender for probity reasons — there, the Brief's value is shaping the brief that gets tendered, not skipping the tender.

## The Facade Resilience Screening (Existing Buildings)

Position the firm as the technical authority an asset owner or ESG/sustainability manager calls _before_ a disclosure cycle or capital-works program forces the question. The gatekeeper here faces a mandatory trigger — most concretely AASB S2/ASRS climate disclosure (facade physical-risk is a genuinely under-assessed line in those reports), or a jurisdictional resilience target on government/statutory assets.

```
[ Facade Resilience Screening ] (Engine-driven, near-zero Director time)
            │
            ├──► Part 1: Current Condition & Compliance Gap
            │         └─► Establishes Technical Authority Ahead of a Disclosure/Capital-Works Cycle
            │
            └──► Part 2: Forward Resilience Radar (Flagged Risks)
                      └─► Creates a Warm, Pre-Briefed Path to Remediation Scope:
                           • 42°C Overheating & Thermal Stress Analysis
                           • Extreme Rain & Box-Gutter Capacity
                           • Hail & High-Suction Structural Wind Loading
                           • Embodied Carbon in Extrusions
```

1. **Part 1: Current Condition & Compliance Gap** Reconstructs the facade's actual current condition and performance against present-day benchmarks (NCC Section J/JV3 thermal bounds, embodied-carbon baseline). Geometry input — as-builts, a site visit, a scan — uses the same rapid reconstruction pipeline as the new-build offering, engineered for the same near-zero Director time at high quality.
2. **Part 2: The Forward Resilience Radar** The same four physical risks as the new-build offering, read against an aging envelope, where they're often sharper: systems weren't designed for today's climate extremes, and some may already be failing quietly.
3. **Commercial Conversion** An unassessed risk gap becomes the case for retaining the firm on remediation and upgrade design — directly where privately owned, and as a pre-briefed advantage into the tender where publicly procured.

**Two things this offering depends on:** (1) the gatekeeper is procurement-bound, not overwhelmed — asset owners are usually already inside a governance process, especially on the government/statutory share of a target firm's pipeline, so "un-tendered" scope is less certain than it sounds: capital works above a threshold typically goes to competitive tender regardless of who wrote the screening; (2) the hook is narrow but sharp — a confirmed AASB S2/ASRS Group 1-reporting owner is rare in this segment, but for that owner, and the wider market of similarly obligated entities, an unassessed physical climate-risk gap in the facade portfolio is a disclosure liability, not a nice-to-have, which probably warrants its own dedicated go-to-market sequencing.

## Target Consultancy Alignment & Rationale

_Applies equally to both offerings above — this is about which firms to take either pitch to, not which offering to lead with._

### Why Tier-1 Multidisciplinary Firms Are Excluded

Tier-1 global practices (Arup, WSP, AECOM, Meinhardt) operate under slow, rigid corporate matrixes requiring multi-layered BD approvals, and — based on nine years running this exact kind of internal ESD/building-physics practice at Arup — will prioritize pitching their own team over adopting an external lead-gen mechanic.

### The Target Profile: Defined by What They Offer, Not Headcount

- **Director-Led Speed:** Principals can approve an automated lead-gen workflow without corporate red tape — a function of decision-making structure, not staff count. A flat, principal-led 90-person firm clears this as fast as a 15-person one; a matrixed 25-person division of a larger group might not.
- **Core Package Vulnerability:** They compete fiercely for structural glass, curtain wall, and envelope packages; winning downstream requires early-stage seats at the architect's table.
- **Physics, Not Rating Administration:** the real dividing line isn't "does the firm mention sustainability," it's whether they staff _scheme administration_ — a GBCA Accredited Professional for Green Star, a NABERS Accredited Assessor for ratings, an energy modeller for a Commitment Agreement — versus compliance-grade _physics_ (NCC/JV3, thermal bridging, wind/water, embodied-carbon quantification). GBCA AP status especially is a wholly separate accreditation from facade engineering registration, with no technical overlap. A firm doing the physics work isn't automatically a conflict for either offering; a firm holding the accreditation is.

**Where certification is genuinely required** — mostly the government/ASRS share of the pipeline — the answer isn't "in-house" or "walk away," it's staying the facade-physics subconsultant and letting someone else own the certification. A Green Star submission can absorb a specialist's thermal/daylight/embodied-carbon inputs without that specialist being the GBCA AP of record; a NABERS Commitment Agreement's energy model needs facade thermal performance as an input but is owned end-to-end by whoever holds the mechanical/whole-building modelling brief; an operational NABERS star rating is pure metered-data administration with no facade content at all. The firm can bill the physics input on every one of these without ever staffing the accreditation side.
