# Differentiation / Superpower

What actually differentiates Jon from other senior technical/sustainability
consultants. Distinct from `CV-Positioning.md` (the general-market framing)
— this is the sharper, comparative version used once the audience and the

## The space this owns
Jon can't win on accreditation density (Green Star, NABERS, WELL) or on
disclosure-framework depth (pure ASRS specialism) — both are crowded,
credential-driven lanes with deep benches already in them (see Geronimo
Advisory's own principal roster). The actual rare space: **an
aerospace-trained systems engineer who moved into building sustainability
and climate resilience.** Formal systems-engineering training and
defence-sector rigour (DSTO), applied to buildings rather than aircraft or
defence systems, is genuinely uncommon in this market — not a stronger
version of what a sustainability consultant does, but a different
discipline's method aimed at the same problem.

This was previously played down: as an ESD consultant, the aerospace
background didn't obviously fit, so foregrounding it felt like a liability.
That changed on identifying **"engineered resilience"** (settled ~a week
before this note, late Sep 2026) as the specific space to make a mark in —
a claim that needs real engineering rigour behind it, which the aerospace/
systems-engineering lineage actually is, not something adjacent or
borrowed. ("Engineered resilience" itself checks out as an established,
~50-year-old term in resilience theory — Holling's original systems-
engineering-rooted concept, distinct from ecological resilience — not
something being coined here.)

## The discipline: systems engineering
The specific vocabulary — abstraction, parameterisation, simplification,
verification, validation — is standard systems-engineering / modelling &
simulation terminology: build a simplified, parameterised model of
something real, **verify** it (was it built right?), **validate** it (is
it the right model of reality?). Comfort working outside a home domain, applied (not
fundamental) research, and integrating disparate tools is what a systems
engineer does by definition — moving across subsystem boundaries,
integrating work that domain specialists built separately. The step before
any of that — the **diagnostic** step — is figuring out what actually
needs modelling, and at what fidelity, before parameterisation starts.

Most senior technical/sustainability consultants are strong on one side of
this: domain physics, or delivery/reporting, or technology-adjacent
strategy — rarely all three, and rarely with the commercial-practice
experience (P&L, service-line ownership) to package it as a sellable
offering. The combination is the differentiator; no single piece of it is
rare on its own.

## The applied capability
- **Physical + financial systems**: thermal, fluid dynamics, wind, hazard
  physics — the mechanism, not just the compliance output — extended into
  financial modelling: cost, capex, risk quantification tied to a physical
  outcome. Not social or political modelling (stakeholder engagement,
  policy) — a different, adjacent skill not claimed here. Actual financial
  scope (self-assessed beginner/intermediate, corrected 24 Sep 2026):
  tariffs/bills, ROI and payback-period calculations (BESS Digital Twin,
  most retrofit work), and Co-Perform-era cost/pricing/profitability
  across service lines — not margin analysis, not banking-level modelling.
  The BESS project may have included tariff-arbitrage functionality —
  **unconfirmed**. No financial modelling in the embodied-carbon or NABERS
  Commitment Agreement work — those were purely technical. This practical,
  downstream-of-the-physics scope is arguably a better fit for
  asset-owner-facing advisory than a more abstract financial claim would
  be.
- **Architecture, not just analysis**: doesn't just run numbers inside
  someone else's tool — architects the data model and analysis model
  itself (schema, calculation logic, the pipeline from raw input to
  decision-ready output). Same instinct behind the Co-Perform SaaS stack
  and BER-Engine.
- **Prototype, then direct the build**: can build a working prototype
  personally, but doesn't have to be the one who builds the production
  version — equally capable of specifying the architecture and directing
  external developers to build it out. Matters commercially: a firm
  doesn't need to fund a full dev team to get value from this.

## Where the translation layer actually lives (25 Sep 2026)
Resilience-engineering theory work (Dekker, Hollnagel, Woods) surfaced a
distinction that reframes this whole document: **climate adaptation**
(updating design loads, retrofitting — real, valuable, defensible
engineering work Jon can deliver anywhere) is not the same thing as
**resilience engineering** (systems thinking, feedback loops tracking
outcomes over time, organisational authority to coordinate a response
across systems when something degrades). The second requires portfolio
reach and operational authority that a single-discipline specialist firm
— or a specialist sustainability practice — doesn't have and can't acquire
by hiring one person.

The actual differentiator this document is describing — the "applied
capability" above, the systems-engineering method, the breadth of physical
systems — is genuinely valuable either way. But the space where it becomes
**true resilience engineering** rather than **very good climate
adaptation** is the "translation layer": portfolio climate risk → asset-
level vulnerability → building physics response → operational continuity →
prioritised capex and evidence. That layer lives in organisations with
existing portfolio visibility, operational reach, and an ESG/climate-risk
disclosure framework already in place — e.g. Anthesis, CBRE, GHD (join
beneath their disclosure/scenario work as the building-physics delivery
layer) or Savills, Acumentis (lead resilience/climate-risk within existing
portfolio/asset management). See [[Job Search Strategy]] for the full
Route 1/Route 3 reasoning. This doesn't change what the superpower *is* —
it changes which conversations can actually cash it in at full value, and
sharpens the confidence-building job (function 1) even further: the gap
Jon spotted 25 years ago is real, it's just in a different sector than
where the JFS pitch first went looking.
