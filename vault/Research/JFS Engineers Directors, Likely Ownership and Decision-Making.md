# JFS Engineers: Directors, Likely Ownership and Decision-Making

## Executive assessment

The most defensible current conclusion is that **Justin Nieuwoudt is the primary commercial and organisational gatekeeper for any Australia-wide engagement or newly created director role**. JFS calls him Managing Director; the company’s own profile says his focus has shifted to growing JFS, supporting clients across regions and shaping team culture. He should be treated as the essential sponsor, even though public material does not establish that he owns a majority—or any particular percentage—of the Australian company.[^1][^2]

**Leo Iebra is likely the most important technical validator and the person whose support would most reduce internal resistance.** He has been Technical Director–Façades since JFS began in February 2022, is a chartered civil/structural engineer, and leads the Canberra contact point. A building-physics, climate-resilience and analytics proposition sits next to his technical authority, so bypassing him would be strategically unwise even if Justin controls the commercial decision.[^3][^4]

**Peter Buffett is the critical Melbourne stakeholder but probably not the original owner or ultimate national decision-maker.** He joined JFS as Director–Victoria only in June 2025 and was publicly described as leading the Melbourne division, client relationships and projects. For a Melbourne-based appointment, his support is operationally important; however, his recent arrival makes it less likely that he is part of the original 2022 ownership structure, unless equity was granted later.[^5][^6]

The actual Australian shareholding **cannot responsibly be inferred from job titles**. JFS Engineers Pty Ltd is an active proprietary company limited by shares, incorporated on 16 February 2022, but free public sources do not disclose its members or share allocation. An ASIC current-and-historical company extract is the decisive next document because it includes current and former officeholders, share structure and member holdings; ASIC currently lists the online fee as $20.[^7][^8][^9][^10]

## Likely ownership hypotheses

Public evidence supports probabilities, not a definitive cap table.

### Most plausible

The Australian company appears to have been built around a small founding leadership group, with Justin and Leo both beginning in February/March 2022 when the entity was incorporated. Justin publicly said in May 2022 that he was “part of the team” heading the development of JFS, wording that implies a multi-person founding or development group but does not identify the shareholders.[^16][^4][^18][^7]

A reasonable working hypothesis is therefore:

- Justin is likely to hold substantial economic and/or governance influence because he is Managing Director and the externally visible growth leader.
- Leo may be a founder, shareholder or key founding executive because his tenure starts with incorporation and he holds the national technical title.
- Robert may have ownership in the separate New Zealand company, but this has not been verified.
- Peter and Troy are less likely to be original Australian shareholders because both became JFS directors in 2025, though either could have received equity as part of joining.[^17][^5]

### Alternative structures

Several alternatives remain credible:

- Justin could be the sole or controlling shareholder, with other “directors” serving as senior executives or regional leaders.
- Justin and Leo could share ownership, equally or unequally.
- An unseen individual, family trust, holding company or industry partner could hold some or all shares.
- Australian, New Zealand and UAE businesses could be separately owned affiliates operating under a common brand rather than subsidiaries of one parent.
- Regional directors could hold minority equity or profit participation without being shareholders in JFS Engineers Pty Ltd.

None of these should be presented to JFS as fact without registry evidence.

## Influence versus ownership

Even after obtaining the cap table, the largest shareholder may not be the only person who matters. For this opportunity, practical decision power is likely divided across four domains:

| Decision domain | Likely lead | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| National strategy and permission to create the role | Justin | Managing Director and publicly identified growth/culture leader.[^1][^2] |
| Technical mandate and professional-risk acceptance | Leo | Technical Director and founding-era technical leader.[^4] |
| Melbourne pipeline, office fit and local utilisation | Peter | Leads the Melbourne division and current Victorian projects.[^6] |
| Cross-Tasman method alignment and overlap management | Robert | Existing building-physics and condensation expertise in the NZ operation.[^12] |

This suggests that a successful director-role proposal requires a **coalition**, not merely a shareholder’s approval: Justin as sponsor, Leo as technical validator and Peter as local operating partner. Robert becomes important if the proposition is branded as a group-wide practice rather than an Australian service line.

## Engagement versus role

### External engagement

For an initial paid advisory engagement, Justin is the clearest buyer because the scope concerns firm strategy, service-line development and growth. The lowest-friction offer would be a fixed, short diagnostic that reviews the live pipeline, identifies three to five pilot services or projects, maps internal capability and produces a 12-month commercial plan.

The engagement should require interviews with Leo and Peter and optionally Robert. This both improves the work and avoids making them feel that a new strategy is being imposed around them.

### Employed director role

For a permanent director/practice-lead role, the decision is more consequential: title, remuneration, revenue expectations, professional liability, reporting line, hiring authority, ownership of methods and possible equity all become relevant. The likely approval path is:

1. Justin accepts the strategic and commercial case.
2. Leo accepts the technical boundaries and governance model.
3. Peter confirms that the person and proposition strengthen Melbourne rather than compete with his remit.
4. The registered directors/shareholders approve contractual and, if relevant, equity terms.
5. Robert is consulted if the mandate extends across the wider JFS brand or uses NZ resources.

The pitch should therefore avoid asking for the fully formed 20-year role at the outset. A stronger route is **engagement first, role option second**: complete a strategic diagnostic, deliver one or two fee-earning pilots, agree performance gates, then convert to a director appointment or national practice mandate.

## Positioning by person

### Justin

Lead with the business case:

> JFS already has the façade credibility and project access. The opportunity is to convert selected building-physics, remediation and climate-resilience work into a repeatable, higher-value national service line—starting with a small number of paid pilots and measurable revenue, margin and cross-sell targets.

The strongest proof points for him are client demand, pipeline conversion, delivery model, margin, recruitment requirements, liability controls and how quickly the capability can become self-funding.

### Leo

Lead with complementarity:

> The aim is not to take building physics away from façade engineering or replace existing technical leadership. It is to deepen the analytics, methods and service architecture around the façade expertise JFS already has, with technical governance designed together.

Give him an explicit role in methods, peer review, sign-off boundaries, software standards and quality assurance. This transforms a potential territorial concern into visible technical sponsorship.

### Peter

Lead with Victorian growth:

> The immediate value in Melbourne is to help win earlier-stage advisory work, strengthen remediation and high-performance design offers, and give your client relationships a differentiated capability that the larger multidisciplinary firms struggle to integrate with specialist façade delivery.

Make clear that he retains Victoria leadership and key client ownership. The proposed role should bring him services, analytical depth and delivery leverage—not compete for his regional position.

### Robert

Lead with shared capability:

> New Zealand already has credible thermal, condensation and weatherproofing expertise. The opportunity is to build common methods, tools and quality systems across the group so that each region can sell and deliver more without duplicating effort.

His expertise should be acknowledged directly. He may be an especially useful ally for testing whether the proposed practice architecture respects current capability.

## Questions for the first meeting

These questions uncover actual decision rights without bluntly asking “who owns the company?” too early:

- “How do strategic investments in new capability normally get decided across JFS?”
- “Would a role like this sit nationally under the Managing Director, within Victoria, or as a group-wide technical practice?”
- “Who would need to be convinced that this was commercially worthwhile and technically well governed?”
- “How are responsibilities divided between the Managing Director, Technical Director and regional directors?”
- “Are Australia, New Zealand and the Middle East separate operating businesses, or do they share ownership, P&L and investment decisions?”
- “Would the preferred path be an initial advisory engagement, an employed practice lead, or a staged route from one to the other?”
- “For a director-level appointment, does JFS use ‘director’ as an executive title, a statutory office, an equity position, or some combination?”
- “What revenue or margin threshold would a new practice need to achieve before you would invest in staff or offshore analytical capacity?”

The ownership question can be made natural once mutual interest exists:

> “To understand how a director-level proposition would be structured, could you talk me through the ownership and governance of the Australian business, and whether regional directors participate in equity or profit share?”

## Due-diligence action

The single highest-value next step is to order the **current and historical ASIC extract** for JFS Engineers Pty Ltd, ACN 657 366 384. ASIC states that this extract includes current and historic directors, members/shareholders and address information, and currently charges $20 online. It should answer:[^10][^9]

- Who the statutory Australian directors and secretary are.
- Their appointment dates and any former officeholders.
- The issued share classes and quantities.
- The current members and recorded holdings.
- Whether an ultimate holding company is recorded.
- Whether ownership or governance changed when later regional directors joined.

The extract is highly probative but not perfect. ASIC records are based on company lodgements, and the company’s own member register remains the underlying record; nominee or trust holdings may also obscure ultimate beneficial ownership. If the extract identifies corporate or trustee shareholders, a second search should follow those entities until the human controllers or the practical limit of public information is reached.[^19]

For the NZ entity, the official Companies Register can disclose directors and shareholdings, and it can be searched by company, director or shareholder name. That search would clarify whether Robert, JFS Engineers Pty Ltd or another party owns JFS Engineers NZ Limited and whether the regional businesses have common control.[^20]

## Recommended approach sequence

1. **Verify the cap table and legal board.** Obtain the historical ASIC extract before negotiating role title, equity or decision rights.
2. **Seek a strategic conversation with Justin.** Present a commercial hypothesis and staged process rather than a pre-written job description.
3. **Ask Justin to bring Leo into technical scoping.** This signals respect for JFS’s existing authority structure.
4. **Include Peter before defining the Melbourne operating model.** Make the local benefit and reporting relationship explicit.
5. **Offer a paid 6–10 week diagnostic or pilot phase.** Define tangible outputs, client opportunities, pilot projects, delivery controls and financial gates.
6. **Pre-agree the conversion decision.** If agreed measures are met, discuss appointment as Director, Building Performance & Climate Resilience, including budget, hiring authority and any equity or profit-share pathway.
7. **Extend group-wide only after Australian proof.** Involve Robert and Troy in common methods and international scaling once the initial offer works commercially.

## Negotiation implications

If the ASIC extract shows concentrated ownership—especially a sole or dominant Justin holding—the proposal can move relatively quickly once he is convinced, but key-person and succession risk should be assessed. If Justin and Leo share material ownership, the pitch must satisfy both commercial growth and technical-governance priorities from the beginning.

If Peter has no equity, “Director–Victoria” is likely an executive title rather than evidence of ownership. In that case, an offered “Director” title may not bring board rights, statutory authority or participation in enterprise value; these need to be negotiated separately. Conversely, if regional directors do hold shares, JFS may have an established pathway to equity that can be discussed after commercial proof.

Any director-role discussion should separate five matters explicitly:

- Executive title and external market positioning.
- Statutory directorship and legal duties.
- Reporting line and decision authority.
- Profit share, bonus or practice P&L participation.
- Equity, vesting, leaver provisions and valuation.

The central strategic recommendation is to pitch **a controlled route to a director role**, not simply the title: paid diagnostic, fee-earning pilots, agreed performance gates, then a national mandate with clearly documented governance and economics. That structure is more likely to appeal to a young, privately held specialist consultancy while allowing both sides to test working relationships and demand before making a large commitment.
