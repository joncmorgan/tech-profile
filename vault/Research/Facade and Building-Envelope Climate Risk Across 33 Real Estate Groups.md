# Facade and building-envelope climate risk across 33 listed and institutional real estate groups

## Scope and method

This report consolidates the facade and building-envelope relevant content of the latest available climate-related disclosures for **33 organisations**: 16 ASX-listed Australian property groups, 16 global listed or institutional real estate groups, and The GPT Group, added from the user-supplied *Climate and Nature Disclosure Statement 2025*.

**Reporting years covered:** 2024 to 2026 reporting years, depending on each group's publication cycle. The Australian cohort is drawn largely from FY25, FY26 and calendar-2025 reports; the global cohort from 2024, 2025 and 2026 sustainability, TCFD, IFRS S2 and CSRD-scope disclosures.

**Disclosure frameworks represented:** TCFD; AASB S2 and the Australian Sustainability Reporting Standards (ASRS), including first-year adopters using the C4(b) Scope 3 transition relief; IFRS S1/S2 (voluntary early adoption); CSRD/ESRS sustainability statements; GRI Standards; SASB; and US Form 10-K climate risk-factor disclosure. Scenario work spans RCP 1.9 through RCP 8.5, the SSP1-1.9 to SSP5-8.5 range, NGFS transition scenarios, IEA NZE 2050, B2DS, STEPS, SDS and 2DS, and CRREM stranding pathways.

**Important interpretive note.** Most REIT climate disclosures describe hazards and mitigations at *asset* level, not at *facade-component* level. The word "facade" appears in a climate-risk context in only a handful of the reports reviewed. Consequently, many rows in the register below are **inferred** from a disclosed hazard (for example cyclone wind speeds, hail severity, extreme heat days, driving rain) or from a disclosed design measure (glazing, shading, insulation, cladding, drainage, whole-life carbon). Every inferred row is labelled — either **(inferred)** for rows sourced from the Australian report or **(inferred from disclosed hazard/measure)** for rows sourced from the global report — and the underlying disclosure is still cited. Cells that could not be confirmed in a fetched document are shown as "n.a." or `n.a.` exactly as recorded in the source research.

## Key findings

- **Explicit facade language is rare across both cohorts.** Only six of the 33 groups tie a climate hazard or response directly to the facade in their own words: Goodman Group ("structural damage to buildings, including roofs, cladding, facades and external infrastructure"), Dexus (facade performance as a decarbonisation lever plus facade-retention adaptive reuse), Abacus Group (a portfolio-wide "Façade integrity program"), Landsec ("The performance of our façade and fabric materials is designed to cope with expected higher and extreme temperatures, as well as increased wind speeds"), Keppel REIT ("Increasing the shading coefficient in facades") and Gecina ("Insulation and glazing performance is specifically challenged when buildings face high indoor temperatures during heat waves").
- **Cyclone and extreme wind is the dominant structural-envelope hazard in logistics.** Goodman names cyclones and extreme wind as "the most significant physical climate hazards" and has "increased the structural strength of our standard warehouse design"; The GPT Group's Townsville logistics asset is "designed and built to wind speeds specific to the region"; Hines lists "Roof Bracing" and "Storm Shutter" among its adaptation features; Abacus Storage King warns that "High winds can lift roofs, shatter windows, damage solar panels".
- **Hail is the most financially quantified peril.** Scentre Group puts hailstorm vulnerability at 12.8% of portfolio book value, or $4,298m at 31 December 2025 — a larger exposure than bushfire (4.5%, $1,525m) or flooding (2.1%, $698m). Goodman states hail "can cause severe damage to roofs and external facades"; Prologis installs "hail guards"; Dexus concedes hail has "not been modelled due to data uncertainty".
- **Heat and solar gain is the most widespread chronic driver of glazing and shading specification.** Keppel REIT specifies "insulated double-glazed curtain wall glass" and window tinting; Gecina qualifies "tinted window glazing or cooling paint"; BXP requires a "high-performance thermal envelope and glass to minimize thermal bridging and solar heat gain"; SEGRO deploys "brise soleil, louvres, window tinting"; Stockland builds a "Performance façade with vertical sun shading"; Mirvac installs window films; The GPT Group relies on "passive cooling techniques" and reflective materials for urban heat island.
- **Water ingress and drainage is the most frequently disclosed envelope failure mode, and the most frequently engineered response.** Scentre reports "Hail and/or heavy rain result in guttering being overwhelmed leading to water ingress"; Mirvac maintains building envelopes explicitly for "extreme rain and hailstorms"; Abacus Storage King warns intense rain "can overwhelm gutters and stormwater drains"; The GPT Group is "upsizing hydraulics"; Link REIT has prioritised 29 Hong Kong assets "for drainage inspection and repair of external pipes"; BXP specifies "Waterproofing of subgrade infrastructure" and "Floodable first floors".
- **The two cohorts diverge on what is forcing envelope investment.** Australian groups are in an early-adoption phase — first-year AASB S2 statements (Goodman, Scentre, Dexus, Lendlease), asset-level climate adaptation plans (Scentre across all 42 Westfield destinations, GPT at 100% of wholly owned and managed Office and Retail, Cromwell at 100% of Australian managed assets), and embodied carbon pilots (Dexus's Embodied Carbon Emissions Framework, GPT's assessments at Rouse Hill Town Centre and 51 Flinders Lane, Mirvac's 25% recycled content target). European groups face hard regulatory drivers already triggering envelope retrofit: MEES EPC B by 2030 (British Land, Landsec, SEGRO), the French DPE marketing bans and *décret tertiaire* (Gecina), German and EU building energy-performance regulation (Vonovia), and explicit whole-life carbon caps in kgCO2e/sqm (British Land below 500 from 2030, SEGRO 139 by 2034).
- **Retrofit capex is quantified only by the European names.** British Land estimates £100m to decarbonise and meet MEES, with £34m spent; Landsec has a £135m Net Zero Transition Investment Plan with £32m invested; SEGRO estimates approximately £55m to bring the UK estate to EPC B; Vonovia commits €400–500m per year to envelope-focused modernisation, "insulating facades, cellar ceilings and lofts, and replacing windows". No Australian, US or Asian group in the set publishes an equivalent envelope retrofit capex figure.
- **Facade retention and adaptive reuse is emerging as the single largest embodied-carbon lever.** Landsec reports structural retention at Thirty High cutting upfront embodied carbon by 60% (347 kgCO2e/sqm); Dexus's Brisbane adaptive reuse retaining "existing structure, lift core and façade" avoided approximately 20,800 tCO2e, and 33 Alfred Street was modernised "with its heritage façade preserved"; Gecina reinvented the Mirabeau facade rather than replacing it; British Land weighs "refurbishment and redevelopment" alternatives in development decisions; National Storage REIT names adaptive reuse of existing assets and materials.
- **Aluminium and glass remain the blind spot in embodied-carbon disclosure.** Concrete and steel dominate: Goodman explicitly concedes "lower-carbon concrete, steel and aluminium are not currently included within our standard design specifications"; Mapletree focuses "specifically on concrete, reinforcement bars, and structural steel"; only BXP names "concrete, steel, and aluminum" together as targeted high-intensity materials, and Mitsubishi Estate records an external-stakeholder view that it "should conduct risk assessments on building materials (particularly steel, cement, and glass)".
- **The disclosure gaps are systematic.** Across all 33 groups there is no reference to AS 1170.2 wind-load design, no Bushfire Attack Level (BAL) rating, no non-combustible cladding commitment, and essentially no salt-corrosion or coastal-durability measure — the closest being Keppel REIT's disclosure of "Damage to properties from exposure to sea spray and water contact". Goodman's "increased the structural strength of our standard warehouse design" is the nearest thing to a wind-design statement in the cohort.

## Engineering implications

**Wind-load and cyclone design is a logistics-sector problem that the sector states in narrative rather than code terms.** Goodman identifies cyclones and extreme wind as "the most significant physical climate hazards" with the potential to damage "roofs, cladding, facades and external infrastructure", and its response is a design-standard change — "In Australia, we have increased the structural strength of our standard warehouse design" ([Goodman TCFD report](https://www.goodman.com/-/media/project/goodman/global/files/sustainability/overview/sustainable-properties/goodman-tcfd-report.pdf)). The GPT Group is the only group in the cohort to describe a specific asset as "designed and built to wind speeds specific to the region", at its Townsville logistics facility ([Climate and Nature Disclosure Statement 2025](https://www.gpt.com.au/)). Hines lists "Roof Bracing" and "Storm Shutter" as building adaptation features ([Climate Strategy Report 2024](https://hines-assets.s3.amazonaws.com/documents/FINAL_Hines_Climate-Report_2024_2025-01-17-154410_zscx.pdf)), and Abacus Storage King warns that "High winds can lift roofs, shatter windows, damage solar panels and cause power outages" ([Sustainability Report 2025](https://cdn.craft.cloud/019ef897-2b1c-7125-bae9-dca0f5d53e9a/assets/ASK-Sustainability-Report-2025_FINAL.pdf)). For a facade engineer, the implication is that region-specific wind speed selection, roof-to-wall tie-down and glazing impact resistance are the live design variables in cyclone-exposed logistics and storage, even though no group in the cohort cites a wind-loading standard.

**Hail drives impact rating for roofs, skylights and translucent sheeting more than it drives vertical facade specification.** Scentre Group quantifies hailstorm vulnerability at 12.8% of portfolio book value, or $4,298m, the largest single peril exposure disclosed anywhere in this cohort ([2025 Annual Report](https://assets.ctfassets.net/0vreywxqlgab/7KwSHYP13rs5SdWMFCbzF8/7748c4f7883ce0c499b0e1c6db07a623/2025_Annual_Report.pdf)). Goodman states hail "can cause severe damage to roofs and external facades" ([2026 Annual Report](https://www.goodman.com/-/media/project/goodman/global/files/investor-centre/gmg-goodman-group/announcements/asx-announcements/2026/2-goodman-2026-annual-report-1.pdf?rev=c6a2112d7f914e1c8d043edf2c132259)). Abacus Storage King locates the damage precisely: large hail "can damage solar panels, skylights, translucent sheeting and block drains" ([Sustainability Report 2025](https://cdn.craft.cloud/019ef897-2b1c-7125-bae9-dca0f5d53e9a/assets/ASK-Sustainability-Report-2025_FINAL.pdf)). Prologis is the only group to name a hail-specific hardware response, installing "hail guards" ([2024/25 TCFD Report](https://prologis.getbynder.com/m/1540200114328c7d/original/2024-25-Prologis-TCFD-Report-FINAL.pdf)). Dexus's admission that hail has "not been modelled due to data uncertainty" ([2026 Annual Report](https://www.dexus.com/content/dam/dexus/documents/corporate/sustainability/sustainability-reports/2026/2026%20Dexus%20annual%20report.pdf)) shows the peril is under-modelled relative to its disclosed financial weight.

**Under heat scenarios the cohort splits between high-performance glazing and external shading, and the two strategies are rarely reconciled.** Keppel REIT specifies "insulated double-glazed curtain wall glass", "Increasing the shading coefficient in facades" and "window tinting with blinds for extra shading" ([Sustainability Report 2025](https://www.keppelreit.com/file/sustainability/sustainability-report/keppel-reit-sr25.pdf)); BXP mandates a "high-performance thermal envelope and glass to minimize thermal bridging and solar heat gain" ([2026 EMS](https://www.bxp.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/2026-BXP-EMS-Program.pdf)). Against that, SEGRO reviews "orientation/window positioning of the building… including external planting to provide shade, brise soleil, louvres, window tinting" ([Responsible SEGRO Report 2024](https://www.segro.com/media/lpkbrx5z/sg100087_responsible-segro-report_2024_interactive.pdf)), and Stockland delivers a "Performance façade with vertical sun shading designed to balance the occupants' needs to access natural daylight and building efficiency by limiting solar gains" ([Annual Report 2025](https://www.stockland.com.au/globalassets/corporate/investor-centre/fy25/fy25/stockland-annual-report-2025.pdf)). Gecina makes the trade-off explicit in process terms: dynamic thermal simulation "must mention the adaptation solutions to be implemented, whether passive (overhangs, solar protections, etc.) or active" and "must also include the effects of urban heat islands" ([2024 URD](https://www.gecina.fr/sites/default/files/2025-03/gecina_universal_registration_document_urd_2024_e-accessible.pdf)). Gecina also identifies the residual vulnerability that low-SHGC glass alone does not solve: "Insulation and glazing performance is specifically challenged when buildings face high indoor temperatures during heat waves."

**Water ingress and drainage capacity is the most commonly disclosed and most commonly under-specified failure path.** Scentre states it plainly: "Hail and/or heavy rain result in guttering being overwhelmed leading to water ingress. This impacts operations and requires building infrastructure upgrades" ([2025 Annual Report](https://assets.ctfassets.net/0vreywxqlgab/7KwSHYP13rs5SdWMFCbzF8/7748c4f7883ce0c499b0e1c6db07a623/2025_Annual_Report.pdf)). Mirvac assigns the function to the envelope itself, which "must be maintained to support continued resilience to extreme rain and hailstorms" ([Climate Resilience Report 2024](https://mirvac-cdn-web.azureedge.net/-/media/project/mirvac/corporate/main-site/corporate-theme/images/sustainability/performance/mirvac_climate-resilience-report-2024_final-for-publication-on-080824.pdf)). The GPT Group is the only group to state a capacity uplift as design practice — "upsizing hydraulics where appropriate to account for increased severity of storms" ([Climate and Nature Disclosure Statement 2025](https://www.gpt.com.au/)). Everyone else addresses water at the ground plane rather than in the envelope: BXP with "Waterproofing of subgrade infrastructure" and "Floodable first floors" ([2026 EMS](https://www.bxp.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/2026-BXP-EMS-Program.pdf)), Gecina with "cofferdams, non-return valves" ([2024 URD](https://www.gecina.fr/sites/default/files/2025-03/gecina_universal_registration_document_urd_2024_e-accessible.pdf)), Centuria with automatic flood barriers rated to 75 cm ([2025 Climate-related disclosures](https://centuria.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/2025-Climate-related-disclosures_FINAL.pdf)), and Charter Hall with 0.5 m freeboard above the 1% AEP ([Sustainability Report 2025](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20250821/pdf/06n4ty3n0qkxwk.pdf)). Link REIT's programme of "drainage inspection and repair of external pipes" across 29 Hong Kong assets ([Climate Resilience and Adaptation](https://www.linkreit.com/en/sustainability/environment/climate-resilience-and-adaptation/)) is the clearest evidence that rainwater goods, not vertical waterproofing, are the failing component.

**Embodied carbon in aluminium, glass and concrete is becoming the binding transition constraint on facade specification.** BXP requires new development to "calculate total embodied carbon (MtCO2e) and embodied carbon intensity (kgCO2e/SF) for the building's baseline structure, foundation, and enclosure, and implement strategies that target a minimum of 14% embodied carbon intensity reduction", naming "concrete, steel, and aluminum" as the high-intensity materials ([2026 EMS](https://www.bxp.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/2026-BXP-EMS-Program.pdf)). British Land is targeting "50% reduction in upfront embodied carbon intensity across our office developments to below 500kg CO2e per sqm from 2030" with an internal levy of £90 per tonne ([CFD 2026](https://www.britishland.com/media/ezobcr2k/bl-tcfd-2026.pdf)); SEGRO is moving from 318 kgCO2e/sqm in 2024 to 139 by 2034 and has already specified "Low-carbon steel for the façade and trapezoidal roofing sheets" plus a "Flexible polyolefin roofing membrane instead of the traditional PVC" at SEGRO Park Frechen, achieving 197 kgCO2e/sqm ([Responsible SEGRO Report 2024](https://www.segro.com/media/lpkbrx5z/sg100087_responsible-segro-report_2024_interactive.pdf)). Goodman states the constraint from the other direction: "lower-carbon concrete, steel and aluminium are not currently included within our standard design specifications" ([2026 Annual Report](https://www.goodman.com/-/media/project/goodman/global/files/investor-centre/gmg-goodman-group/announcements/asx-announcements/2026/2-goodman-2026-annual-report-1.pdf?rev=c6a2112d7f914e1c8d043edf2c132259)). Lendlease adds a market dimension, warning of "'green premiums' for low embodied carbon materials and rising compliance costs for traditional, carbon-intensive materials" ([Annual Report 2026](https://www.lendlease.com/siteassets/lendlease/shared/investor-centre/announcements/asx/2026/08/260817.pdf)).

**Facade retention is now the highest-leverage single move available to a development team, and it is beginning to carry a commercial premium.** Landsec reports that structural retention cut Thirty High's upfront embodied carbon by 60%, to 347 kgCO2e/sqm against 522 kgCO2e/sqm at Timber Square ([Annual Report 2025](https://content.landsec.com/media/3gvc3pxx/annual_report_2025_interactive_0_0.pdf)). Dexus quantifies a Brisbane adaptive reuse retaining "existing structure, lift core and façade" as avoiding approximately 20,800 tCO2e, and preserved the heritage facade at 33 Alfred Street ([2026 Annual Report](https://www.dexus.com/content/dam/dexus/documents/corporate/sustainability/sustainability-reports/2026/2026%20Dexus%20annual%20report.pdf)). British Land formalises the choice — "Development decisions incorporate the environmental impacts of alternative schemes, including refurbishment and redevelopment" — and reports BREEAM Outstanding space commanding a "greater than 10% rental premium above prime Central London office rents" ([CFD 2026](https://www.britishland.com/media/ezobcr2k/bl-tcfd-2026.pdf)). Lendlease points at the design corollary, listing "removable facades" among its construction innovations ([Annual Report 2026](https://www.lendlease.com/siteassets/lendlease/shared/investor-centre/announcements/asx/2026/08/260817.pdf)), and Gecina demonstrates the retained-facade upgrade path at Mirabeau, where the existing tartan facade was "reimagined with an extruded white mesh… through integrated sunshades" ([2024 URD](https://www.gecina.fr/sites/default/files/2025-03/gecina_universal_registration_document_urd_2024_e-accessible.pdf)).

**Energy-performance regulation, not physical risk, is what actually triggers envelope capital works.** The quantified retrofit programmes in this cohort are all regulatory: British Land's £100m to decarbonise and meet MEES with £34m spent ([CFD 2026](https://www.britishland.com/media/ezobcr2k/bl-tcfd-2026.pdf)), SEGRO's approximately £55m to bring the UK estate to EPC B ([Responsible SEGRO Report 2024](https://www.segro.com/media/lpkbrx5z/sg100087_responsible-segro-report_2024_interactive.pdf)), Landsec's £135m Net Zero Transition Investment Plan ([Annual Report 2025](https://content.landsec.com/media/3gvc3pxx/annual_report_2025_interactive_0_0.pdf)), Vonovia's €400–500m per year "insulating facades, cellar ceilings and lofts, and replacing windows" ([Sustainability Statement 2024](https://report.vonovia.com/2024/q4/app/uploads/Vonovia_SE_Annual_Report_2024-Sustainability-Statement.pdf)), and Gecina's renovation of four buildings holding "86% of E EPCs" before the 2034 marketing ban, "involving the building envelope (e.g. double glazing in three buildings)" ([2024 URD](https://www.gecina.fr/sites/default/files/2025-03/gecina_universal_registration_document_urd_2024_e-accessible.pdf)). Outside Europe the equivalent levers are code and certification rather than capex: National Storage REIT cites NCC Section J and the "selection of appropriate building fabrics, glazing and shading, sealing" ([Sustainability Report 2025](https://app.sharelinktechnologies.com/announcement/asx/624b57b0628f7f00893936bf5dc9ff0b)), and Mitsubishi Estate targets an 85% ZEH share by fiscal 2030 "by upgrading the insulation performance of properties" with a "highly insulated exterior shell" ([climate change page](https://mec.disclosure.site/e/sustainability/activities/environment/climate-change/)).

**Adaptation planning is maturing faster than adaptation engineering.** Scentre has Climate Change Adaptation Plans at all 42 Westfield destinations, with detailed engineering adaptation plans noted for 19 of them ([2025 Annual Report](https://assets.ctfassets.net/0vreywxqlgab/7KwSHYP13rs5SdWMFCbzF8/7748c4f7883ce0c499b0e1c6db07a623/2025_Annual_Report.pdf)); The GPT Group has 100% coverage for wholly owned and managed Office and Retail assets, aligned to ISO 14090:2019, ISO 14091:2021 and AS 5334-2013, with less than 3.5% of portfolio value at moderate Value-at-Risk and no high-VAR assets ([Climate and Nature Disclosure Statement 2025](https://www.gpt.com.au/)); Cromwell has decarbonisation plans for 100% of Australian managed assets ([Annual Report 2025](https://www.cromwellpropertygroup.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2025/08/CMW-Annual-Report-2025.pdf)); URW commits to "100% of our exposed assets to implement risk mitigation measures by 2030" ([Better Places 2025 Overview](https://assets.eu.ctfassets.net/1e76kztii87u/6cP9z8ahmkgx3ka3D5EjBm/ccef7dfd72ddc302c63387e2755d5204/Better_Places_2025_Sustainability_Performance_Overview__26032026.pdf)). Vicinity, by contrast, concedes that "Further work needs to be done to understand, quantify and qualify impacts at centres" ([Sustainability Supplement 2024](https://www.vicinity.com.au/assets/sb/f/129601/x/d220097ed5/2024-annual-report-sustainability-supplement.pdf)). Abacus Group's portfolio-wide "Façade integrity program", in which "All building façades were inspected, with all material risks identified with an action rectification plan" ([Sustainability Report 2025](https://abacusgroup.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/ABG-Sustainability-Report-2025.pdf)), is the only condition-assessment programme in the cohort that operates at the facade-component level and is therefore the closest existing analogue to what climate adaptation of the envelope will require.

### Substantive disclosure gaps across the cohort

- **No wind-load standard is cited anywhere.** No group in either cohort references AS 1170.2 or an equivalent wind-loading standard. Goodman's "increased the structural strength of our standard warehouse design" and The GPT Group's Townsville asset "designed and built to wind speeds specific to the region" are the closest statements to a wind-design disclosure.
- **No Bushfire Attack Level (BAL) rating is disclosed**, despite bushfire being a modelled hazard for Scentre (4.5% of book value, "exposure to radiant heat and ember attack"), Mirvac, Charter Hall, Region Group, Growthpoint, Centuria, Ingenia and Vicinity. Mirvac's commitment to "employing best practice bushfire building codes, addressing risks through design and materials selection" is as specific as the cohort gets.
- **No non-combustible cladding commitment appears in any report**, even though cladding is named as a cyclone and hail damage pathway by Goodman.
- **Salt corrosion and coastal durability is almost entirely absent.** Keppel REIT's "Damage to properties from exposure to sea spray and water contact" is the only disclosure in the cohort that touches it, despite widespread sea-level-rise and coastal-inundation modelling.
- **Aluminium and glass embodied carbon is under-disclosed relative to concrete and steel.** Only BXP names aluminium alongside concrete and steel as a targeted material; Mitsubishi Estate records only an external-stakeholder expectation that it assess "steel, cement, and glass"; Goodman names aluminium only to note its exclusion from standard specifications; Lendlease's glass reference is recycled glass used as concrete aggregate rather than glazing.
- **Hail is financially material but methodologically weak.** Scentre quantifies it as the largest peril in its portfolio while Dexus excludes it from modelling entirely "due to data uncertainty".
- **Facade-level condition and performance data is essentially unreported.** Only Abacus Group discloses a portfolio-wide facade inspection programme; no group reports facade thermal performance, air-tightness test results, glazing SHGC or U-value distributions, or facade remaining-life data.
- **Physical Value-at-Risk is unevenly disclosed.** Quantified figures exist for Scentre (peril-specific book value), Dexus (site count, share of value, AFFO threshold), The GPT Group (less than 3.5% at moderate VAR), National Storage REIT (5% by 2030 rising to 12% by 2100), British Land, Landsec, SEGRO and Gecina; but Value-at-Risk is `n.a.` for Mirvac, Stockland, Vicinity, Growthpoint, Centuria, Ingenia, Abacus Storage King, Abacus Group, Prologis, Hines, Brookfield, Link REIT, CLI, Keppel REIT, Mitsubishi Estate and URW.
- **Envelope retrofit capex is disclosed only in Europe.** British Land, SEGRO, Landsec and Vonovia publish figures; no Australian, US or Asian group in the set publishes an equivalent envelope retrofit capex number.
- **Several groups disclose hazards without any engineering response.** Vicinity states that "Further work needs to be done to understand, quantify and qualify impacts at centres"; Region Group discloses eight hazard categories under RCP 8.5 with no design-level response; Simon Property Group names hazards in risk-factor language with envelope engineering measures recorded as `n.a.`
