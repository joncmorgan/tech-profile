# JFS Engineers Opportunity: Strategic Conversation Summary

## Purpose of this document

This document summarises the discussion regarding a potential opportunity to create and lead a new role at JFS Engineers, an Australian façade-engineering consultancy.

The central opportunity is to build a commercially viable, technically rigorous, and strategically differentiated practice around:

- Building physics
- Façade performance
- Sustainability
- Climate-change adaptation and mitigation
- Building and asset resilience
- Whole-life façade performance
- Analytics, modelling, data, and scalable delivery systems

The proposed role is not simply a technical modelling position. It is a potential practice-building and organisation-shaping leadership role that could help position JFS as a specialist “climate-ready façade” consultancy for the next 20 years.

## 2. Strategic Opportunity

### Core assessment

JFS appears to have a strong foundation in façade engineering, structural certification, weatherproofing, construction support, remediation and diagnostics.

However, its public-facing market position appears more mature around:

- Façade structural engineering
- Compliance and certification
- Weatherproofing
- Façade-system engineering
- Project delivery
- Diagnostics and remediation

Its building physics and ESD capability is publicly stated, but appears less visibly developed as a dedicated, branded, analytics-led, national specialist practice.

This creates a possible strategic opening.

The opportunity is to develop a specialist practice that makes JFS the firm clients engage when they need a façade that does more than comply today. The proposition would be that JFS can help clients understand whether their building skins will remain comfortable, durable, low-risk, low-carbon and resilient under changing climate conditions over the life of the asset.

### Recommended positioning

> JFS can become Australia’s leading climate-ready façade consultancy: independent, evidence-led, constructible, and accountable for envelope performance across design, delivery, remediation and asset life.

A useful external positioning line could be:

> We engineer façades for the climate they will experience—not the climate assumptions of the past.

### Strategic differentiation

The distinctive position would sit between:

- Large multidisciplinary engineering firms with broad but sometimes less façade-specialist capability
- Conventional façade consultants focused mainly on systems, details, delivery and certification
- Generic ESD or sustainability consultants who may not have deep façade, moisture, constructability and remediation expertise

JFS could own the interface between:

- Façade structural performance
- Weatherproofing
- Building physics
- Energy and thermal comfort
- Condensation and moisture management
- Climate risk
- Durability and materials
- Carbon and circularity
- Existing-building remediation
- Whole-life asset value

## 3. Proposed Role

### Recommended title

The strongest suggested title is:

> Director, Building Performance & Climate Resilience

Other viable options include:

- Head of Building Physics, Sustainability & Adaptation
- Principal, Façade Performance and Resilience
- Practice Lead, Climate-Ready Envelopes

The preferred title is broad enough to convey director-level commercial responsibility, technical authority, sustainability relevance, adaptation leadership and a practice-building mandate.

### Proposed role purpose

> Build JFS’s national capability in measurable façade performance, building physics, climate-risk adaptation and whole-life envelope resilience—integrating advanced analysis into façade engineering, remediation and client decision-making.

### Core responsibilities

The role should own five connected areas.

#### 1. Technical excellence

Build and govern robust methods for:

- Thermal analysis
- Hygrothermal analysis
- Condensation risk
- Thermal bridging
- Solar control
- Daylight
- Comfort
- Overheating
- Airtightness
- Energy performance
- Future-climate assessment
- Moisture management
- Durability and exposure analysis
- Embodied and whole-life carbon

#### 2. Commercial service development

Create clearly defined, repeatable services with:

- Identified client types
- Standard scopes
- Transparent deliverables
- Fee structures
- Proposal templates
- Appropriate project-stage triggers
- Cross-selling pathways into current façade and remediation work

#### 3. Strategic differentiation

Help reposition JFS from:

> A façade engineer that can undertake some analysis

to:

> A specialist façade-performance and climate-resilience consultancy that can quantify, design, test, certify and manage envelope performance over the asset life.

#### 4. Scalable delivery platform

Create a controlled hybrid operating model in which:

- Senior judgement, client advice, review, methodologies and formal responsibility remain in Australia
- Repeatable analytics production and data work can be supported through an offshore delivery pod
- JFS retains intellectual property, client relationships, quality assurance and technical authority

#### 5. Internal capability building

Train and support JFS directors, façade engineers, designers and project staff so that performance-led thinking is embedded across the company.

The aim is not to create an isolated modelling silo. It is to make building performance part of how JFS scopes, designs, reviews, remediates and delivers projects.

## 5. Operating Model

### Hybrid in-house and offshore model

The proposed approach is to retain high-value judgement and professional responsibility in-house, while using a carefully controlled offshore model for scalable, repeatable analytics work.

### Functions that should stay in Australia

Australian-based senior staff should retain responsibility for:

- Client engagement
- Scope definition
- Project strategy
- Site and façade-system interpretation
- Engineering and architectural judgement
- Modelling assumptions
- Boundary conditions
- Scenario selection
- Technical review
- Client recommendations
- Design integration
- Project meetings
- Certification and formal opinions
- Report sign-off
- Professional accountability
- Method development
- Training
- Quality assurance

### Functions that could be supported offshore

A controlled offshore production team could support:

- Model geometry preparation
- Data preparation
- Repeatable façade-zone analysis
- Parametric runs
- Sensitivity analysis
- Calculation templates
- Visualisation and graphics
- Drawing support
- Schedules
- Report formatting
- Literature and standards extraction under direction
- Dataset development for portfolio screening
- Quality-control checks against defined templates

### Non-negotiable controls

The model should include:

- JFS ownership of all intellectual property
- JFS ownership and control of all client data
- Formal confidentiality and subcontracting controls
- Project-specific data-security protocols
- Named Australian technical lead for every job
- Independent peer review for significant work
- Version-controlled methods and templates
- Clear documentation of inputs, assumptions and outputs
- Offshore work limited to controlled production tasks
- No offshoring of final technical interpretation, professional judgement or sign-off
- Measurement of first-pass quality, rework, turnaround time, technical review outcomes and client satisfaction

### Recommended implementation approach

Start small:

1. Build a small internal centre of excellence.
2. Select one repeatable service, such as thermal bridging or condensation analysis.
3. Establish a controlled offshore production pod.
4. Use it on a small number of projects.
5. Measure quality, margin, delivery speed and client value.
6. Expand only after evidence of success.

- Form a national Building Performance & Resilience practice.

## 7. Political and Organisational Considerations

### Core conclusion

There is no clear public evidence that JFS currently has a named, dedicated:

- Head of Building Physics
- Sustainability Director
- Climate Resilience Lead
- Operational Energy Lead
- Façade Carbon Lead
- Analytics Practice Director

This is the apparent organisational whitespace.

However, existing directors and technical staff may already see elements of building physics, thermal analysis, condensation, weatherproofing and sustainability as part of their current expertise and client territory.

The opportunity must therefore be positioned as **additive and enabling**, not corrective or territorial.

### People most likely to perceive overlap

| Person / stakeholder | Likely sensitivity | Recommended response |
|---|---|---|
| Leo Iebra, Technical Director | Most direct overlap with building physics, façade systems, weatherproofing, technical standards and specialist analysis | Engage early; make him a technical sponsor or co-authority for standards |
| Justin Nieuwoudt, Managing Director | Controls firm strategy, investment, market positioning and likely sees adjacent service lines as part of JFS’s core business | Lead with commercial outcomes, differentiation, margin, risk management and growth |
| Robert Dawber, Director, New Zealand | Publicly associated with building physics, thermal, condensation and weatherproofing work | Treat him as a regional peer and collaborator rather than someone to be overridden |
| Peter Buffett, Director, Victoria | May see performance, sustainability and resilience as a strong Victorian market-development opportunity | Position the practice as a tool that strengthens his Melbourne client work |
| Existing façade engineers and consultants | May fear loss of technical work, client contact or authority | Make the practice an enablement layer that supports project leaders rather than replacing them |

### The most important wording issue

Avoid saying:

> “I don’t think JFS does much of this in-house.”

Even if the public evidence suggests the capability is not currently highly visible as a standalone practice, that wording would risk:

- Challenging current directors’ competence
- Triggering defensiveness
- Implying that previous work has been inadequate
- Suggesting a takeover of existing technical territory
- Reducing willingness to sponsor the role

Use this framing instead:

> JFS already has strong building-physics capability embedded within façade engineering. I see an opportunity to make that capability more systematic, earlier in the design process, more scalable and more visible in the market—especially around climate resilience, adaptation and whole-life façade performance.

### Recommended division of responsibilities

| Area | Existing directors / project leads | Proposed new role |
|---|---|---|
| Façade engineering | System selection, detailing, constructability, structural performance, certification, project leadership | Integrate measurable performance and climate-resilience analysis into decisions |
| Weatherproofing | Design review, test strategy, site judgement, certification and diagnostics | Quantify moisture, condensation and future-climate exposure; create performance standards |
| Client relationships | Lead accounts, win work, manage projects | Add specialist performance advice and create new service opportunities |
| Building physics | Current project-specific input and technical review | Develop national methods, tools, QA, analytics platform, training and specialist depth |
| Sustainability | Existing support where relevant | Productise façade carbon, adaptation, resilience and whole-life performance |
| Remediation | Diagnose defects and design remedial outcomes | Add climate vulnerability, deep-retrofit business cases and adaptation roadmaps |
| QA | Discipline-specific technical review | Performance-analysis governance, modelling QA and specialist peer review |

### Preferred operating principle

> Shared technical authority, differentiated accountability.

This means:

- Existing directors keep authority in their established technical and client domains.
- The new role owns the depth, systems, specialist analytics, methods, training, quality framework and commercial development of building performance and resilience.
- Project directors remain client owners and project leaders.
- The practice becomes a horizontal national capability supporting all offices and project teams.

## 12. Main Risk to Avoid

The largest strategic risk is becoming the person who performs complex analysis late in the project, after:

- Façade systems have already been selected
- Budgets have already been set
- Major geometry decisions are fixed
- Procurement pathways are locked
- Cost-down substitutions are underway
- Defect or comfort problems are already occurring

That creates a reactive, low-influence, fee-pressured support role.

The proposed practice must be embedded at three high-leverage points:

1. **Before concept selection**  
   Set measurable performance targets and evaluate options.

2. **Before procurement and contractor appointment**  
   Test systems, details and substitutions against performance, resilience and whole-life requirements.

3. **Before or during remediation investment decisions**  
   Diagnose actual risk, quantify adaptation needs and build the commercial case for upgrade.
