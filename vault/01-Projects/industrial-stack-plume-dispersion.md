# Industrial Stack Plume Dispersion & CASA Airspace Assessment (TAPM)

## Context
Environmental compliance and aviation safety modelling to determine whether industrial exhaust stack emissions create thermal buoyancy, vertical velocity, or turbulence hazards that penetrate protected aviation airspace or violate regulatory ground-level criteria. Undertaken using CSIRO's TAPM (The Air Pollution Model) around the era of regional dispersion modelling transitions (alongside tools such as AUSPLUME).

## Execution
- Atmospheric Boundary Layer (ABL) Physics: Modelled the atmospheric boundary layer profile by establishing surface roughness lengths across varying surrounding environments (such as built-up residential areas versus open terrain), capturing how local roughness alters velocity gradients and atmospheric dispersion.
- Meteorological Analysis: Analysed historical meteorological datasets to identify prevailing wind regimes, mean wind speeds, and adverse atmospheric conditions (including atmospheric temperature inversions).
- Dispersion & Trajectory Modelling: Utilised TAPM to calculate plume trajectories, dispersion geometry, and maximum plume rise heights across a defined matrix of wind speeds and stability classes.
- Aviation & Building Downwash Criteria: Evaluated stack design parameters, including stack height and discharge exit velocity, to assess critical thresholds: ensuring exit velocity prevents building downwash/cavity recirculation, while verifying whether the buoyant plume trajectory breaches maximum allowable heights or penetrates aviation flight corridors under Civil Aviation Safety Authority (CASA) guidelines, specifically protecting Obstacle Limitation Surfaces (OLS).
