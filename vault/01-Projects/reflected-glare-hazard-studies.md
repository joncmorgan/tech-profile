# Reflected Glare Hazard Studies & Numerical Hassall Automation

## Context
Mitigation of specular reflections and veiling glare from architectural glazing and metallic cladding impacting drivers, transport infrastructure, and surrounding urban streets. Historically, assessments relied on the Hassall methodology—a manual, highly time-consuming geometric drafting method to determine whether reflected sunlight falls within an observer's sensitive optical line of sight. In the early 2000s, spearheaded the development of an automated, numerical equivalent to replace manual calculations and evaluate driver hazard risks across major transport infrastructure projects (including Level Crossing Removal Project road overpasses, rail corridors, and new railway stations such as Williams Landing) and dense urban developments.

## Execution
- Numerical Translation of Hassall Methodology: Transformed the manual Hassall geometric guidelines into a numerical algorithmic workflow, quantifying the angular separation between reflected sunlight vectors and an observer's or driver's central line of sight to identify the threshold of disabling veiling glare.
- Simulation Automation (Rhino 3D & Radiance): Scripted custom computational workflows in Rhino 3D coupled with the Radiance synthetic imaging engine. Automated the generation of synthetic visual fields and luminance distributions sequentially along defined transit corridors and urban streetscapes through 3D models.
- Hazard Quantification: Calculated veiling glare intensity, contrast ratios, and exposure durations directly from simulated luminance maps, evaluating potential visual impairment and safety risks for road vehicle drivers, train operators, and pedestrians.
- Architectural Envelope Optimisation: Applied numerical glare evaluations to inform facade glass selection (reflectance limits), envelope geometry, and external shading design to mitigate hazardous reflections into adjacent public zones and transit corridors.
- Reporting Artifacts: Produced structured technical reports, visual mapping diagrams, and communication artifacts for transport infrastructure and property development clients. Built the automated Radiance-based numerical glare-calculation workflow from scratch — to Jon's knowledge this approach had not been done before at Arup. Whether it continued to be used there after he left has not been verified.
