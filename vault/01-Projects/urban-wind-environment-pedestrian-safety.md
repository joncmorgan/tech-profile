# Urban Wind Environment, Pedestrian Safety & CFD Validation

## Context
5 to 10 years of specialised work spanning BMT and Arup across the UK and Australia. The work occurred during the key industry transition in the early 2000s when numerical CFD was emerging to challenge physical boundary layer wind tunnel testing, which had historically been the only trusted method for pedestrian wind risk assessment.

## Execution
- Wind Tunnel Testing & Validation Studies: Participated in physical wind tunnel testing at BMT—setting up scale models, generating atmospheric boundary layers using upstream roughness blocks, and taking static pressure sensor measurements at ground level to measure peak gusts. Conducted formal validation studies running concurrent numerical CFD models alongside physical wind tunnel tests to benchmark and verify the accuracy of the CFD approach.
- Boundary Layer Physics & Grid Resolution: Solved complex atmospheric boundary layer mechanics in CFD. Managed the technical balance between hardware compute limits and the high grid resolution required to accurately capture upstream boundary layer shear profiles, flow separation, and fluid acceleration around building corners.
- Turbulence Modelling Limitations: Navigated fundamental turbulence modelling constraints. Overcame the limitations of steady-state Reynolds-Averaged Navier-Stokes (RANS) models—which average out turbulence and fail to resolve peak gusts—by implementing transient methods such as Large Eddy Simulation (LES) to assess dynamic peak gust forces capable of causing pedestrian instability.
- Statistical Wind Climate Analysis: Synthesised numerical and experimental velocity data with historical meteorological wind probability records to calculate peak gust wind loads on pedestrians and assess safety and comfort against Lawson and Penwarden criteria.
- Architectural Mitigation Design: Applied results to guide the architectural design, sizing, and positioning of pedestrian-level canopies, podium setbacks, and porous screens to alleviate wind issues on medium- and high-rise developments.
