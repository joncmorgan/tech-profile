## Best first conversations

## 1. Geronimo Advisory

This is your best near-term **Director/Principal or practice-building** prospect. It has a public Principal-led structure, practical existing-building orientation, climate-resilience work that includes engineered retrofit, and a public Principal role focused on direct work with property owners and asset managers.linkedin+2

Your pitch should not be “hire me to do modelling.” Offer a bounded commercial proposition:

> Build a Climate-Resilient Buildings service that identifies which existing assets are vulnerable to future heat, wind-driven rain, overheating, moisture, façade deterioration and storm conditions; converts those findings into prioritised upgrade scopes and capex pathways; and leaves Geronimo with reusable tools, templates and delivery capability.

A sensible entry structure would be a 90–120 day service-line pilot:

- Package the offer, methods, pricing and proposal language.
    
- Build a lightweight, local-first internal decision tool rather than a generic dashboard.
    
- Jointly target two to three owner/asset-manager opportunities.
    
- Deliver or co-deliver the first paid commissions.
    
- Agree in advance on outcomes: associate relationship, Principal/director role, a licence, revenue-share, or internal handover.
    

## 2. Climate Risk Advisory

CRA is likely the best **B2B licensing and delivery partnership** target. Its platform demonstrates willingness to sell a repeatable risk product, while its stated offer already reaches individual assets and site-specific assessment.climateriskadvisory.com+1

Its likely commercial limitation is that a hazard platform can answer “what is exposed?” more readily than “what should this owner physically do to this particular building, in what sequence, for what performance result?” That is the bridge you can own.

A narrowly framed proposition:

> “I can add an asset-to-intervention module to your climate-risk workflow: a building-physics-informed vulnerability screen, adaptation-option library, evidence trail, and decision output that helps advisory teams move from hazard mapping to client-ready retrofit and resilience recommendations.”

Do not lead by proposing a large new SaaS product. First test whether the firm has recurring client requests that currently require bespoke analysis or get handed to external engineers. Then offer a paid co-design pilot for one hazard type and one asset cohort—for example overheating and façade/roof thermal resilience in existing commercial property, or wind-driven rain and water-ingress resilience.

## 3. Inhabit

Inhabit is technically the closest match to your building-physics/envelope identity: façade performance, ESD, building physics and high-performance envelopes already exist in the practice. It also has historical proof of software-linked innovation via the InEnergy smart-façade tool.inhabitgroup+2

However, it is not an independent boutique: Egis has held a majority interest since 2019, and the brand is now operationally aligning with Egis support systems. That introduces both opportunity—resources, client access, international technical depth—and risk—slower approvals, parent-group client conflicts, less flexible IP ownership.inhabitgroup+1

Approach the Australian Building Physics + Sustainability leadership with a very specific service gap:

> “Future-climate envelope resilience for existing assets: diagnostic modelling, thermal/moisture/rain risk, adaptation pathways, remediation brief support and a reusable project decision engine.”

Before sharing valuable tool architecture or code, establish:

- Whether the Australian group can contract a specialist associate or license externally developed tools.
    
- Ownership of pre-existing IP, project-specific configurations, derivative works and client data.
    
- Whether you can exclude defence, nuclear and data-centre assignments categorically.
    
- Whether the role is genuinely senior and market-facing rather than a backfill for project-delivery production.

## A concise positioning statement

Use language like this in initial approaches:

> I lead climate-resilient building-performance work at the point where hazard analytics, building physics and owner investment decisions meet. I combine nearly 30 years in thermal, wind, façade and comfort analysis with full-stack software development and prior consulting-business leadership. Rather than producing another climate-risk report, I build repeatable decision systems that take an owner from future climate exposure to technically credible, prioritised interventions for a specific asset or asset portfolio.

That formulation clearly distinguishes you from:

- Portfolio analytics firms that do not enter the building.
    
- Conventional ESD teams focused mainly on compliance or ratings.
    
- Pure façade engineers focused on current-condition detailing.
    
- Software vendors without engineering accountability.
    

The key commercial thesis is not that every firm needs another climate-risk dashboard. It is that many advisory teams already identify climate exposure, while relatively few can reliably translate it into **specific physical interventions, performance outcomes, costs, priorities and defensible advice for the asset owner**. Your building physics plus systems/software capability is unusually well suited to own that translation layer.
