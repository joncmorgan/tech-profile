# Job Search Strategy

Not a single job search — three parallel routes, run at different intensities,
not a single linear plan. This doc exists so a change to one route (or to
the CV) doesn't quietly drift out of step with the others.

## Values screen (applies to every route)
See [[Career Screening Tool]] (03-Values). No target — company, role, or
sector — gets pursued without passing this first.

## Climate adaptation vs. resilience engineering (25 Sep 2026 — cuts across every route below)
Rigorous work-through of resilience-engineering theory (Dekker, Hollnagel,
Woods) surfaced a distinction that had been conflated up to this point:
- **Climate adaptation** — updating design loads, retrofitting for future
  conditions. Real, valuable, defensible engineering work.
- **Resilience engineering** — systems thinking, visibility across
  multiple building systems, feedback loops tracking outcomes over time,
  organisational authority to coordinate a response when something
  degrades. Structurally requires portfolio reach and operational
  authority — something a single-discipline specialist firm doesn't have
  and can't acquire by hiring one person.

Test case: the Richmond window film project solved the technical problem
(the modelling) but had no loop asking "is this actually working?" or
"what happens in 5 years?" — that missing loop is resilience. Nobody paid
for it because the immediate legal exposure was already solved. That's the
concrete evidence the distinction is real, not just theoretical hair-
splitting.

**Practical implication**: a specialist firm (façade, single-discipline
consultancy) can genuinely host climate-adaptation work. True resilience-
engineering work — the translation layer from portfolio risk signal to
asset-level building response to operational continuity — needs an
organisation with portfolio visibility, operational reach (works with
building managers, not just designers), an ESG/climate-risk disclosure
framework already in place, and authority to coordinate across systems.
This reshapes how Routes 1 and 3 below should be pitched and prioritised.

## Foot in the door — priority strategic principle (26 Sep 2026)
Explicit reframe: the goal for Routes 2 and 3 is **not** to land a Director-
level mandate on day one. With the CV as it currently stands, that's a low-
probability play against candidates who already hold the accreditation/
portfolio-scale track record these firms are advertising for. The goal is
to get **inside** one of these organisations — any credible technical
role, any reasonable title — on the reasoning that proximity beats
positioning: once inside, promotion, internal mobility and the chance to
build/propose a new service line all become dramatically easier than they
are from outside. Jon has confirmed he can absorb a pay cut and has no
attachment to seniority of title to make this work.

**Refined 26 Sep 2026 — how this actually plays out**: not by applying to
listed senior roles (a 52-year-old competing against a 30-year-old on a
generic ATS screen for a titled vacancy is a losing play regardless of the
CV). The realistic path is the network — surfacing an Associate Director/
Associate-level opening (or similar) through a warm contact who already
knows Jon's background, not a stranger reading a CV cold. That changes
what the CV needs to do: with someone who already knows him, it's a
confirmation/leave-behind document, not a screening filter, so **one CV
is enough** — no consulting-specific fork needed (resolves the "single CV
vs. consulting variant" open question below). The pitch in that
conversation is explicit and doesn't need softening: **a low-cost,
experienced, motivated person who can be trusted to grow quickly into a
future Director-level role** — the network contact already knows the
track record, so the value proposition is speed-to-competence and
retention risk (motivated to stay and grow), not "convince them I'm
capable." This sharpens the "applied capability" pitch already in
`Differentiation-Superpower.md`.

**Practical effect on Routes 2/3**: sort every target into **entry point**
(ask, through a warm contact, for whatever role is actually available, at
whatever level) versus **destination** (the Director/Principal-level role
that's 1-3 years further out once established inside). Don't hold out for
the destination role as the opening move, and don't treat a cold listed-
job application as the primary mechanism — the network conversation is.
- **Entry points (apply/ask now)**: Aurecon via Steve Downing/Nick Lander
  (whatever's open near that team, regardless of title); Arup via Joan Ko;
  Deloitte via Will Symons → Fahim Tonmoy — relationship-led, title

This doesn't replace Route 1 (JFS) — a paid-pilot proposition is its own
