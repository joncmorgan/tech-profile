Australia is now hiring for the **portfolio-and-disclosure end** of climate resilience more visibly than for a standalone “future-climate building-physics” discipline. The clearest current opportunities combine climate-risk/scenario analysis, ASRS/ISSB reporting, ESG asset-management services, and business development; the envelope, overheating, flood and wind engineering layer is usually embedded within broader sustainability, façade, technical-advisory, or resilience teams rather than named directly in the job title. [careers.savills.com](https://careers.savills.com.au/en/job/493728/national-director-head-of-esg-asset-management)

For someone with your building-physics, façade and practice-building background, the commercial gap is real: owners and advisers can increasingly identify climate exposure, but need credible pathways from a portfolio risk flag to an asset-specific scope, capex plan, performance target, operational response and retrofit decision.

## Where the technical gap remains

### What buyers say they need

Australian policy and client demand increasingly point to a concrete asset-performance agenda:

- The Commonwealth’s built-environment plan calls for buildings that are liveable, thermally comfortable and resilient to hazards across their life, including retrofit of existing commercial, public and residential buildings. [treasury.gov](https://treasury.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-09/p2025-698821.pdf)
- Australia’s National Adaptation Plan points toward future-climate considerations in the NCC, including future weather files, heatwaves and thermal-comfort metrics, along with climate resilience within rating schemes and energy-performance programs. [unfccc](https://unfccc.int/sites/default/files/resource/NAP_Australia_2025.pdf)
- Western Australia’s 2026 technical guideline is unusually explicit: map heat, flood and wind hazards; prioritise life safety, operational continuity and passive resilience; assess future climatic conditions; and use dynamic thermal simulation for comfort over the asset life—including heatwaves. [wa.gov](https://www.wa.gov.au/system/files/2026-07/tg050-building-resilience-to-climate-change.pdf)

These sources validate the underlying technical need: **future-weather building simulation, envelope and shading assessment, cooling-load reduction, passive survivability, flood/wind/bushfire interfaces, critical-services continuity and adaptation-capex prioritisation.**

### What organisations buy today

The purchasing vehicle is usually one of these:

1. **Disclosure and investor defensibility**  
   Asset owners need climate-risk governance, scenario analysis and auditable disclosure under ASRS/ISSB-linked obligations. The engineering evidence is often subcontracted, embedded or absent.

2. **Asset-value and insurance protection**  
   Owners, lenders and insurers want a defensible view of exposure, financial materiality, resilience priorities and adaptation expenditure. Acumentis’s offer explicitly uses flood, bushfire and extreme-heat stress testing in this way. [esg.acumentis.com](https://esg.acumentis.com.au/location/sydney/)

3. **Operational performance and cost reduction**  
   Property managers buy energy management, building optimisation, ratings and reduced lifecycle cost. This is highly visible in Savills’ senior ESG mandate. [careers.savills.com](https://careers.savills.com.au/en/job/493728/national-director-head-of-esg-asset-management)

4. **Retrofit, remediation and due diligence**  
   Building consultancies buy assessments that lead to a scope: façade upgrades, shading, glazing, cooling, drainage, waterproofing, roof, plant, controls or operational contingency planning. CBRE’s role signals this combination of audit, due diligence, net-zero and resilience work. [careers.cbre](https://careers.cbre.com/zh_CN/careers/JobDetail/Senior-Sustainability-Consultant/262737)

The market weakness is that these procurements often stop at a hazard score, disclosure narrative or generic adaptation recommendation. Your strongest proposition is therefore not “I conduct climate-risk assessments.” It is:

> “I convert a physical-risk finding into an asset-specific, future-climate-tested intervention pathway: what fails, who is exposed, which envelope/plant/operational levers matter, what to prioritise, what it is likely to cost, and what evidence supports the investment decision.”

That is more differentiated than an ESG dashboard, and more commercially intelligible than selling simulation alone.

### Insurance and finance angle

The insurer/reinsurer market is more likely to name the work **physical climate risk**, catastrophe risk, exposure management, climate-risk modelling, resilience, adaptation, or climate/sustainability risk—rather than thermal comfort or façade performance. That creates a partnership opportunity: insurers understand accumulation and loss; building-physics specialists can quantify building-level failure modes, passive survivability, intervention effectiveness and upgrade requirements.

The Resilient Building Council’s network illustrates this crossover. It includes climate/sustainability-risk practice leadership advising insurers, banks, investors, asset owners and governments, alongside building-resilience researchers and disaster-resilience specialists. [rbcouncil](https://rbcouncil.org/our-team/)

## Compensation and market signal

Public Australian advertisements rarely disclose salary for director-level climate/buildings roles, so there is not enough current listing evidence to state a precise employer-by-employer pay figure.

The market signal is more useful than a generic salary estimate:

- **Director/Technical Director roles** are paid for a mix of technical authority, profitable service-line development, sales, executive client credibility and people leadership—not merely modelling capability. Anthesis, GHD and Savills all make growth, market development and leadership central. [careers.savills.com](https://careers.savills.com.au/en/job/493728/national-director-head-of-esg-asset-management)
- **Asset-management ESG leadership** commands value where the holder is responsible for national service delivery, performance outcomes, risk mitigation, investor interface and team management. [careers.savills.com](https://careers.savills.com.au/en/job/493728/national-director-head-of-esg-asset-management)
- **Specialist building-risk expertise** is most likely to command a premium when attached to large portfolios, transaction/due-diligence decisions, insurer/lender evidence, or a recurring managed service—not standalone project-by-project simulation.
- In your case, the appropriate commercial framing is a **practice-building Director/Principal mandate**: build a climate-resilient-assets offer, generate pipeline, set technical standards, recruit/coach a team, and convert portfolio risk into engineering and operational programmes. A conventional “Senior Sustainability Consultant” scope would materially under-use your experience.

## Bottom line

The Australian demand is genuine, but it is currently **buyer-led by compliance, asset value, operational performance and financial-risk management**—not by an abstract desire for climate-resilient architecture. The visible senior vacancies say employers want leaders who can turn ASRS/ISSB, ESG, investor, insurance and resilience pressures into profitable client services. [careers.savills.com](https://careers.savills.com.au/en/job/493728/national-director-head-of-esg-asset-management)

Your differentiated space is the missing translation layer:

\[
\text{Portfolio climate risk} \rightarrow \text{asset vulnerability} \rightarrow \text{building physics and envelope response} \rightarrow \text{operational continuity} \rightarrow \text{prioritised capex and disclosure evidence}
\]

That gives you two viable routes:

1. **Join or lead a climate-risk/ESG advisory practice** and build the asset-technical delivery capability beneath its reporting and scenario-analysis work.
2. **Create a resilient-assets / future-climate building-performance practice** within a façade, building-science, property technical-advisory or asset-management firm—selling to ESG advisers, REITs, fund managers, insurers and major property managers as a specialist delivery partner.

The second route is less likely to appear as a neatly advertised Australian role. It is therefore best pursued as a proposition-led Director/Principal appointment, where the title is negotiated around the revenue line: **Director, Climate Risk & Building Performance; Director, Resilient Assets; Technical Director, Climate Resilience & Building Performance; or Principal, Physical Climate Risk & Adaptation.** Those are sensible *proposed* titles, while the market’s existing nomenclature remains the titles listed above.
