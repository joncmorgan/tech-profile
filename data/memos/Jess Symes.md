
# Meeting Debrief: Discussion with Jess Symes (Climate Zero)

## Market Dynamics & The Role of Consultants

- Climate Zero Platform: Climate Zero is a platform designed to aggregate climate data, manage Science Based Targets (SBTi), handle reporting, and assess risks.  
- The Partner Model: Their business model relies heavily on consultant partners, such as Rewild, who use the platform to generate numbers and fulfill ASRS reporting requirements for clients. 
- Outsourcing Trend: Large Group 1 and Group 2 entities are highly unlikely to bring climate data management in-house. Instead, large Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs) are engaging consulting firms like Rewild to handle this work.  

## Data Provenance & Accountability Concerns

- The "Garbage In, Garbage Out" Dilemma: A major concern is data reliability. Climate data coming from building management systems and various properties is often messy, aggregated, and lacks a "rock-solid" foundation or validation stamp.  
- Director Liability: Despite potential issues with data quality, CFOs and directors will soon be required to sign off on these reports. They will be held to the same level of accountability as traditional financial reporting.  
- The Financial Analogy: In financial reporting, source data (from the ATO or bank accounts) is virtually unimpeachable. Climate data has no such standard validation.  
- Safe Harbor Cushion: There is currently a Safe Harbor period for a few years before strict "reasonable assurance" kicks in. During this time, directors must show "positive accounting"—meaning they can vouch that, to their knowledge, nothing in the data is wrong.  

## The Skills Gap in ESG Consulting (Accountants vs. Engineers)
- Lack of Technical Expertise: Traditional accounting firms are staffed with CPAs who understand dollars and cents. Conversely, ESG consulting firms are not necessarily stacked with people who understand kilowatt-hours, greenhouse gas emission factors, or gigajoules of gas. They are heavily relying on the software platform to do the heavy lifting.  
- The Equivalence: This is comparable to an accountant relying blindly on software without actually understanding foreign exchange, accounting rules, or how the Reserve Bank of Australia (RBA) operates.  
- The Need for In-House Engineers: While ESG firms might think processing utility bills is simple, technical expertise becomes critical when addressing the core pillars of ASRS/ISRS reporting—specifically Metrics & Targets and Strategy.  

## Strategy & The Four Pillars

- ASRS/ISRS reporting requires organizations to address four main pillars: Governance, Risk, Metrics & Targets, and Strategy.  
- Governance & Risk: Can likely be handled by climate scientists, sustainability consultants, or those who manage the organization's standard risk register.  
- Metrics & Strategy: If a large portfolio owner needs a roadmap to Net Zero by 2050, a NABERS 5.5-star pathway, or an electrification strategy, a sustainability generalist will be out of their depth. This work fundamentally requires an engineer.  

## Career Implications & Next Steps
- The Consultant Dilemma: If ESG firms realize they need engineers for the strategy pillar and begin hiring them, the risk is ending up back in a traditional engineering consulting firm, which may not be the desired outcome.  
- Moving Away from Spreadsheets: Many large Group 1 organizations are currently relying on spreadsheets and are realizing they need to transition. This leaves them with two choices:  
- Outsource the work to external consultants, resulting in six-figure annual consulting bills.  

## Bring the capability in-house.  
- The In-House Value Proposition: An in-house role would involve cutting out the external consultant by managing the software platform (like Climate Zero) directly, acting as the resident engineer and data provider. However, current market indicators suggest these large organizations prefer to outsource rather than hire internally, leaving the exact direction of this career opportunity uncertain.
